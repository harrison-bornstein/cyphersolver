# 7-4-4 fol. 28-30: Sigismund August to Ibrahim Pasha, Cracow, 1 June 1548. Decipherment with R367

Files: `744_latin.txt` (fol. 30r exemplar), `744_A_signs.txt` (fol. 28, full sign transcription, this pass),
`744_dupl_signs.txt` (fol. 29, the maintainer's labels with an independent check of L01 and L11-L13),
`744_reading.txt` (token lines 744A, 744D).

Image limit: all work on the 932 px scans in `ais/` (deskewed, cropped and enlarged). The 2400 px AIS scan could not be
fetched (the proxy refused archivinformationssystem.at). Inside the circle-on-stem family (ϙ, δ, δ̶, ♀) this resolution
does not always separate the shapes, so those values are partly chosen by the word. That is a key ambiguity (the key
gives two values), not the crib supplying a value outside the key. Words where it decided the reading are listed below.

## What fol. 28 enciphers (the main question)

fol. 28 enciphers the **same letter as the exemplar, heading included**, with few nulls. It is not a longer text. There are
16 cipher lines plus an interlinear header line. Excluding null words, that is about 553 key signs plus about 15
loose-letter nulls. The maintainer's estimate of about 19 lines and 1,000 signs is too high.

- Interlinear header (L00): "Ad H(e)brai Bassa(r)", the exemplar's address line, enciphered (n, d, z and cum are nulls).
- L01: "Exemplum literarum <Regis Poloniae>": the exemplar's "Exemplum literae Regis Poloniae", enciphered.
  It has "literarum" for "literae", and "Regis Poloniae" is the code word Vb, written "Bu" by this hand.
- L02-L16: "Sigismondus Augustus Dei gratia <Rex Poloniae> magnus dux Lituuaniae, Rusiae, Prusiae, Masoviae et[c].
  Dominus et haeres. Magnifice domine amice noster dilecte. Misimus ad Serenissimum et potentissimum Caesarem
  Turcharum cum literis hunc interpretem nostrum Nicolaum Armenium; a magnificentia vestra postulamus, pro ea amicitia
  quae nobis cum Maiestate illius intercedit, omni consilio et auxilio illum adiuuet quomodo possit ad Caesaream
  Maiestatem tuto peruenire; rem nobis gratam faciet, quae bene valeat. Datum Crachouiae prima mensis Iun.
  Millesimo quingentesimo quadragesimo octauo, Regni vero nostri primo. Et loco suo. Ex comisione Maiestatis Regiae
  propria."

English, both copies: Sigismund Augustus, King of Poland, Grand Duke of Lithuania, Ruthenia, Prussia and Masovia, to
Ibrahim Pasha, his dear friend. The king is sending his interpreter Nicolaus the Armenian, with letters, to the Sultan.
He asks the Pasha, for the friendship between the king and the Sultan, to help the interpreter with advice and
assistance so that he can reach the Sultan safely. This will please the king. Given at Cracow, 1 June 1548, in the
first year of the reign, by the king's own command.

## Sign variants (this hand; fol. 28 unless stated)

- δ (circle with a loop below, the "looped g"): d in ad, Dei, Dominus, domine, dilecte, quadragesimo; e/ae in
  Exemplum, Dei, Rusiae, haeres, magnifice, Caesarem, ea, quae, Regiae. It is the key's "circle on stem with a second
  small circle" (e/ae) and also the "looped g = d" variant. The two cannot be told apart at 932 px. Once (illius) the
  shape stands for u.
- δ̶ (the same shape with a bar through the stem) = t: gratia, dilecte, interpretem, Maiestate, possit, tuto, Maiestatis.
- ϙ (plain circle on stem) = u/v, but also e (noster, vestra, nostrum) and t (noster, postulamus). Because of the
  fragment family, t is often ♀ drawn small.
- Πg, the footed pi = g (Augustus, magnus, magnifice, magnificentia, gratam, Regni, Regiae). In quingentesimo and
  quadragesimo, g is written as Π followed by a Γ-like foot (ΠΓ). It is read as one sign here. If the foot is a
  separate Γ (i), both words gain an extra i ("quingientesimo", "quadragiesimo").
- U (cup with a taller left upright) = g (Sigismondus, gratia), as the maintainer found on fol. 29.
- Plain long-top Π = s (Sigismondus, Augustus, Prusiae, Misimus, mensis, maiestatem, Maiestatis, nostri). The small
  lowercase π without the long top is the null.
- r has three shapes: Latin e, ꝛ (2-shaped: interpretem, rem, nostri, propria) and ꝗ (circle with a crossed tail:
  literarum). x is the 9 with a smooth tail (Exemplum, dux, ex). This agrees with the maintainer's fol. 29 correction
  (crossed tail = r, smooth tail = x) and with k367.txt ("9 = x"). Only KEY_BRIEF.md's wording has them the other way
  round.
- ᵽ (P with a circle or foot below) = o (noster, consilio, quomodo, tuto, octauo, loco, comisione). P = n. °P = o.
- 7 = i, and = ll (the double) in illius, illum, Millesimo. o7 and Γ = i.
- ꝏ (taurus-like) and α = q. Δ = et throughout (fol. 29 uses ∇ and once spells out "atque").
- λ = h, also in the "ch" spellings Turcharum and Crachouiae, which both copy the c-λ pair.
- Code word Vb is written "Bu" (B, then a u-like hook), twice: L01 "literarum <Regis Poloniae>" and L02 "Dei gratia
  <Rex Poloniae>". fol. 29 spells "rex ... Poloniae" out and uses no code word.
- Nulls on fol. 28: idcirco, Dar, cum, spu (3), spo (2), atq (2), and loose b, d (2), n (3), z (9), π (2). A cursive d
  (∂ with a tilde) stands between Exemplum and literarum, read as the null d. "zz" after Δ in L03 may stand for the
  "c" of "&c.".

## Disagreements with the exemplar (fol. 30r)

fol. 28 (744A):
- The heading is enciphered (interlinear L00 and L01). fol. 29 does not encipher it.
- "Hebrai": the e is a δ written above the λ. If that stroke belongs to the docket line above, the cipher has "Hbrai".
- "Bassa" + a final Latin e with a superscript cross (e = r): the signs give "bassar". Left unread as [bassar].
- "Exemplum literarum" for "literae". "Regis Poloniae" and "Rex Poloniae" are both code (Vb).
- The spellings Sigismondus, Lituuaniae, Rusiae, Prusiae, Turcharum, Crachouiae and comisione are read as spellings,
  each sign on its key value.
- "magnus": one sign (ϙ?) stands for "nu", so the signs give "mag(u)s". Corrected to {magnus}.
- "Masoviae": the signs give m-o-s-a-u-i-e. fol. 29 has the same order, so the slip was probably in the scribes' copy
  text. Corrected to {Masoviae}.
- "&c.": only Δ (et) plus two z nulls.
- "potentissimum": the signs give "potentissum". Corrected to {potentissimum}.
- "interpretem": the signs give "iterpretem" (no n). Corrected to {interpretem}.
- "peruenire": the signs give p-e-u-e-n-i-r-e, with a small e written above the δ and no r. Corrected to {peruenire}.
- The exemplar's "qua bene valeat" is "quae" here. The exemplar's "Junij" is "Iun" here (o7 ϙ h, then a small π, the
  null), taken as an abbreviation.
- "Maiestatis Regiae propria" is spelt out. Where the exemplar has "Prebria" (maintainer's reading), the signs give
  "propria".
- The plain text otherwise matches word for word, including "quomodo" and "pro ea", and adds no "Dominum".

fol. 29 (744D), checked against the maintainer's transcription:
- It opens with "replica" (e circ-ring T cup o7 cross2 hash = r-e-p-l-i-c-a, all key values), then the nulls π and n,
  then "Sigismundus". "Replica" stands where fol. 28 has "Exemplum literarum Regis Poloniae": the duplicate labels
  itself as a replica.
- "rex" is followed by M (a), which is not explained, and then "Poloniae" spelt out.
- {Lituaniae}: the signs give lit-u-r-aniae (9tail = r).
- {Masoviae}: as fol. 28.
- It adds "Dominum" before "Caesarem".
- It has "litteris" (tt), "atque" for "&", and "quo" for "quomodo".
- {Maiestate}: one circle-on-stem sign stands for "ie".
- {Caesaream}: the c-lat sign stands for r.
- {Maiestatem}: no m, no i.
- "qua" (as the exemplar).
- **The L11-L12 date clause resolves.** After "octauo" come the nulls Ctail and π (each followed by a full stop). Then
  "Regni" (circ-x #o pi-feet h 7flat). The maintainer's two "I-foot" signs are °P (o) and P (n), so the signs give
  "vero" (o- K circ-x °P) and "nostri" (P d6 a t-loop e-lat 7). "primo" is missing (the line ends before the edge).
  L12 gives "et loco suo", the nulls, "ex", c-o-m-i-s-i-s-o-n-e ({commissione}, with i-s/s-i transposed; the
  maintainer's "T-foot" is °P = o), then **"sacrae regiae"**. The maintainer's "Gamma F~" is one wide Π with a stub
  (s): Π = + e-lat circ-ball gives s-a-c-r-ae, and circ-x K pi Gamma circ-ball gives r-e-g-i-ae. L13 continues
  "Maiestatis propria". So fol. 29 ends "ex commissione sacrae regiae Maiestatis propria", where fol. 28 has "ex
  comisione Maiestatis Regiae propria". No signs are left unexplained in L11-L13.

## The fol. 29 heading question

"eg T ϙ ∞ ‡ # Π . n . ПΓU7aFϙhθ∂...": yes, it resolves. Read as e δ T ∪ o7 ‡ # (the "ϙ ∞" of the question is the cup ∪
= l and o7 = i), it gives r-e-p-l-i-c-a = **"replica"**. Then "Π . n ." are the nulls π and n. Then
"Sigismundus" begins (a Γ U 7 a F ϙ h θ ∂... = s-i-g-i-s-m-u-n-d-u-s; note that fol. 29 has u where fol. 28 has
Sigismondus with °P). The exemplar's heading "Ad Hebrai Bassa. Exemplum literae Regis Poloniae" is not enciphered
on fol. 29. On fol. 28 it is.
